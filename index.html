About My Work

Designing learner-centered AI, pedagogical agents, and learning environments to empower the next generation.

I am an Artificial Intelligence in Education (AIED) researcher and Ph.D. Candidate in Curriculum & Instruction at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (DELTA Research Group), advised by Prof. H. Chad Lane.

My research sits at the intersection of the learning sciences, artificial intelligence, and human-computer interaction. With my background in both computer science and learning sciences, I design, build, and empirically investigate pedagogical agents and interactive learning technologies in authentic K–12 settings—including classrooms and summer camps. My work centers on understanding how intelligent, learning experiences can actively reveal and cultivate students' computational thinking, problem-solving persistence, and scientific reasoning competencies.

Prior to UIUC, I earned my B.A. in Computer Science with a Minor in Mathematics from Colorado College (Major GPA: 4.0, Cumulative GPA: 3.93), where I was an NCAA Postgraduate Scholar and Team Captain of the Men's Varsity Tennis team.

Focus & Methodology

Research Vision & Themes

My work develops computational foundations and empirical frameworks to build pedagogical agents that are relatable, pedagogically grounded, and supportive of diverse learners.

Theme 01

AI-Powered Pedagogical Agents for K–12 STEM

Designing embodied, conversational agents that provide timely, personalized scaffolding in authentic digital STEM learning environments (such as Minecraft and VEXcode VR). Using neural networks, computer vision, and learning science driven AI approaches to detect student goals, offer adaptive feedback, and sustain interest.

• In-game virtual near peers for science inquiry
• In-game virtual agent for scaffolding learning-by-building
• Deployed in informal after-school and summer camp settings
Pedagogical Agents AI in Education Personalized Learning
Theme 02

Learning Analytics & Problem-Solving Strategies

Investigating how learner behaviors reflect computational thinking and non-cognitive skills. Using educational data mining approaches (e.g., sequential pattern mining, clustering, etc.), my research models the distinction between productive persistence and "wheel-spinning" in STEM learning environments, enabling intelligent systems to intervene precisely when needed.

• Learning analytics
• Persistence vs. wheel-spinning behavioral patterns
• Real-time behavioral triggering systems
Educational Data Mining Multimodal Analytics Personalized Support
Theme 03

Teacher-Informed Design & Relatable Agents

Developing authoring toolkits (the INVITE Pedagogical Agent Toolkit) and environments with LLM simulated students that empower educators and novice designers to customize pedagogical agent appearances, gestures, and pedagogy through participatory design. Investigating how learner perception of agent relatability influences social, behavioral, and cognitive reception.

• Teacher-in-the-loop participatory design
• Toolkits for PA authoring
• Simulations for PA authoring
INVITE Pedagogical Agent Toolkit Authentic AI Simulations for Education Human-AI Interaction
Theme 04

Multimodal Assessment & Generative Feedback

Bridging multimodal analytics with natural language processing (NLP) to evaluate open-ended student creations. On WHIMC we combined deep learning vision models, NLP, and log data to automatically assess student-generated scientific observations and Minecraft builds. In INVITE, I have extended my work with multimodal data to assess and give feedback on student open-ended creative artifacts.

• MineObserver 2.0 in-game multimodal pipeline
• Minecraft Mars habitat assessment pipeline
• BarrelBot LLM feedback evaluation on student puzzles
Multimodal Analytics AI for Coding and Scoring LLM Feedback
